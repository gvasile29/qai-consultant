# Proven Playbook: What Top Marketers Actually Do

Evidence-based tactics from 2025-2026 industry data and how leading dev-tool / B2B SaaS brands operate. Use these to make choices defensible, not intuitive. Sources listed at the end.

## Copywriting frameworks (use, don't wing it)

QAI's problem-first rule maps directly onto the **PAS** framework, which is why it works. Named frameworks the best marketers hybridize:

- **PAS (Problem, Agitate, Solution)** - our default. Name the pain, make it sting a little, then resolve with QAI. Best for the hook.
- **AIDA (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action)** - good full-post skeleton: sharp hook, quick context, tie to a concrete benefit, direct CTA.
- **4Ps (Promise, Picture, Proof, Push)** - strong for release posts: promise the outcome, paint the before/after, prove it (deterministic/grounded), push the CTA.
- **Elite move:** hybridize. PAS for the hook, FAB/4Ps for the body, AIDA for the close. Don't force one formula end to end.

## The hook is everything (and it's measurable)

- **Only ~125 characters show before the "...more" cut** on LinkedIn. The scroll-stopper must land in the first line, before any reader clicks. Never bury the hook under a greeting or "Excited to announce."
- Provocative statement or a genuine question outperforms announcements. "Excited to share v3.5" is the weakest possible opener.
- Use a curiosity gap: promise meaning/resolution the reader has to keep reading to get. QAI's "the document nobody's brave enough to reopen" is a curiosity gap plus a pain, that's why it works.

## Format choices, ranked by data

- **LinkedIn text posts** remain the highest-performing format for B2B: the algorithm distributes them widely, they feel personal not promotional. Default to a well-structured text post for most QAI content.
- **Carousels win on engagement and saves.** LinkedIn carousels get ~1.3-1.8x the engagement of text posts. On Instagram, carousels hold the highest engagement of any format (~10% vs ~6% for regular reels) and the most *saves*, and the algorithm gives them a "second chance" (shows slide 2/3 later if slide 1 didn't land), yielding 2-3x impressions. For educational QA tips and data-heavy breakdowns, prefer a carousel.
- **Instagram carousel sweet spot: 8-10 slides, single 4:5 aspect ratio throughout.** Mixing image + video slides beats image-only.
- **Employee/founder content gets ~8x the engagement of company-page content.** Gabi posting as a person (building in public) will always beat a faceless brand page. Write in first person where it fits.

## Cadence and timing (from LinkedIn 2025-2026 benchmarks)

- **2-3 substantial posts per week beats daily superficial ones.** The algorithm rewards value density, not volume.
- **Tuesday-Thursday, mid-week** sees the highest engagement.
- Platform engagement rose ~44% YoY (avg ~3.85%); a founder with a modest following can realistically expect meaningful reach on a strong post. Set expectations accordingly, one good post beats five filler ones.
- **Community over followers:** replying to every comment in the first hour compounds reach. Building an engaged community beats accumulating passive followers. Treat the comment section as part of the post.

## How leading dev-tool brands actually win (models to copy)

- **Supabase** - memes as a lead magnet to pull ICP developers into following on X. Personality and humor lower the barrier; the meme is the top of funnel. QAI can use light, insider QA humor (the "green dashboard nobody reads") without becoming a joke account.
- **Cloudflare** - transparent technical blogs, free tools, and *themed launch cycles* ("Security Week", "Zero Trust Week"). Bundling releases into a themed week manufactures momentum. QAI could theme a run of posts (e.g. an "Audit-Readiness Week").
- **Twilio** - developers empowered through APIs, tutorials, hackathons, the "Ask your developer" campaign. Education is the marketing.
- **Evan You (Vue/Vite)** - the creator visibly hands-on in public (GitHub issues, explaining decisions) carries more weight than any press release. Gabi's "built with Claude Code, here's the design decision" behind-the-scenes posts are exactly this lever.
- **Common thread across all winners:** educate developers, reduce onboarding friction, build community, reinforce credibility through transparency and real product value. QAI already has the transparency (EU AI Act, deterministic, cited sources), lean on it as a *marketing* asset, not just a compliance one.

## The credibility engine for QAI specifically

- **Educate, don't advertise.** The steady drumbeat of educational QA tips (what makes a test flaky, what an audit checks, why risk needs evidence) builds the authority that makes the occasional release post land. This is the single highest-ROI, lowest-cost content stream.
- **Reduce friction in every CTA.** "Free, in-browser, no install" and the one-line MCP install command are conversion levers, always make the next step trivially easy.
- **Transparency as differentiation.** In a market full of AI tools that hallucinate, "deterministic, cited, EU AI Act compliant" is a genuine moat. Say it plainly.

## Quick decision rules (bake into drafting)

1. Educational tip or data breakdown → carousel (LinkedIn or Instagram).
2. Release / milestone / behind-the-scenes narrative → text post, first person.
3. Always front-load the hook in the first ~125 chars.
4. Founder voice over brand voice wherever authentic.
5. One clear CTA, made frictionless.
6. Reply to comments fast; the post isn't done when it's published.
